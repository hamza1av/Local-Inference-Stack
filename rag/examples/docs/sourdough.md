# Sourdough Bread Notes

## The starter

The starter is a mix of rye and wheat flour at 100% hydration (equal weights of flour and
water). It lives in the fridge between bakes.

### Feeding schedule

Take the starter out of the fridge the evening before baking. Discard all but 20 g and
feed it 50 g flour and 50 g lukewarm water. It is ready when it has doubled and a spoonful
floats in water, usually after 6 to 8 hours at room temperature.

## Dough

A good everyday loaf: 500 g bread flour, 350 g water (70% hydration), 100 g active starter
and 10 g salt. Higher hydration gives a more open crumb but is harder to shape.

### Bulk fermentation

Do four sets of stretch and folds, 30 minutes apart, then leave the dough covered until it
has grown by about 50% and shows bubbles on the sides. At 24 °C this takes around five
hours; in a cold kitchen it can take twice as long.

### Shaping and cold proof

Pre-shape into a loose round, rest 20 minutes, then shape tightly and place seam-up in a
floured banneton. Proof overnight in the fridge for 12 to 16 hours. The cold proof makes
scoring easier and adds flavour.

## Baking

Preheat the oven with a Dutch oven inside to 250 °C for 45 minutes. Bake 20 minutes with
the lid on, then 25 minutes at 230 °C with the lid off until the crust is deep brown. Let
the loaf cool for at least an hour before cutting it.

## Troubleshooting

A dense, gummy crumb usually means under-fermentation: give bulk fermentation more time.
A flat loaf that spreads out means over-proofing or weak shaping.
